# Daily plan: the 09:30-10:30 Big Picture hour

The hour on the calendar, for each day is mainly JUST for **big picture retrieval, refactoring, re-planning** ... in agent-speak, it's about refreshening or making statefullness current ... it is not intended for details such as first contact with new code. ## The assumption ledger

One file, `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, appended every Saturday. Every row: the assumption, where it
enters (chapter, equation, lab), whether it is testable with your data, and what would
falsify it. By week 15 this file is the honest core of your final report.

# Readings

Text sources only. Each entry says **what to read it for**, because a bibliography without a purpose is a reading list for someone else.

Order matters: the numbered items in each week are read in that order. If you are behind, read the first item and skip the second — the first is load-bearing.

## Part I — Provenance

### Week 1

1. **PROV-AGENT**, §1–2 — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866>
   Read for: the motivating failure (an agent's output becomes another agent's input) and the claim that existing provenance does not capture agent-centric metadata. Note every sentence that asserts causation.

2. **From Agent Traces to Trust** (survey), §1 — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990>
   Read for: the taxonomy of the field — evidence attribution, tool-use safety, memory, observability. This is the map you will use to place your project.

### Week 2

1. **W3C PROV Primer** — <https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/>
   Read for: `Entity`, `Activity`, `Agent`, and the four relations. Do the primer's examples by hand; the notation is simpler than it looks and the intuitions are load-bearing for week 6.

2. **PROV-O ontology** — <https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/>
   Read for: reference only. Look up a relation when the primer is ambiguous; do not read end to end.

3. **PROV-AGENT**, §3 — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866>
   Read for: which entities are added (`AIModelInvocation`, `AgentTool`) and what `agent_id` links. Verify the names against the paper's schema table, not against this syllabus.

### Week 3

1. **Flowcept docs: instrumentation** — <https://flowcept.readthedocs.io/>
   Read for: the decorator surface and the difference between function-level and workflow-level capture. If a decorator name differs from the syllabus, the docs win.

2. **Flowcept paper** (e-Science 2023) — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09004>
   Read for: why message queues and in-situ analysis were chosen. It explains why capture is asynchronous, which explains the flush delay you will meet on day 3 of the lab.

3. **MongoDB docs, "Read" section** — <https://www.mongodb.com/docs/>
   Read for: only the query operators you need for week 4. Resist the tutorial.

### Week 4

1. **Flowcept docs: querying / analysis** — <https://flowcept.readthedocs.io/>
   Read for: how tasks are identified and how parent/dependency fields can be traversed.

2. **Survey, §3** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990>
   Read for: what the field considers a "reconstruction" and which questions it thinks traces answer.

### Week 5

1. **Survey, §§5–6** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990>
   Read for: the explicit gap list — claim-level support, memory invalidation, responsibility. This is the raw material for your audit report's second half.

2. **PROV-AGENT, §7** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866>
   Read for: the authors' own limitations. Compare with the survey's list; where they disagree, that disagreement is an exam question.

## Part II — Causal identification

### Week 6

1. **A Crash Course in Good and Bad Controls** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06108>
   Read for: the six canonical DAGs (confounder, mediator, collider, and their controls). This is the single most useful paper in the course; read it twice, once before week 6 and once in week 12.

2. **Pearl, Glymour & Jewell, *Causal Inference in Statistics: A Primer*** — companion material at <http://bayes.cs.ucla.edu/PRIMER/>
   Read for: chapter 1–2 on structural models and d-separation. Do the exercises; the DAG reading skill is not transferable from prose.

### Week 7

1. **Hernán & Robins, *Causal Inference: What If*** — <https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/causal-inference-book/>
   Read for: the target-trial framing and the confounding chapters of Part I. Their insistence on "a well-defined intervention" is the discipline this course is trying to install in agent experiments.

2. **dagitty** — <https://www.dagitty.net/>
   Read for: the adjustment-set feature and its output format. Learn to disagree with it: it answers a graph question, not a modelling question.

### Week 8

1. ***Causal Inference: What If***, counterfactual chapters
   Read for: the three-step counterfactual and the difference between a counterfactual and a prediction.

2. **DoWhy documentation** — <https://www.pywhy.org/dowhy/>
   Read for: the four-step API (model, identify, estimate, refute). Read the refutation section first; it is the honest part.

### Week 9

1. **causaloptim** — <https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/causaloptim/>
   Read for: bounds as linear programs over a causal graph. Even if you do not run R, the vignette's worked example is the clearest explanation of why bounds are wide.

2. ***Causal Inference: What If***, the identification framing (re-read with bounds in mind)
   Read for: what "identified" means well enough to recognise its absence.

### Week 10

1. **PROV-AGENT, in full** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866>
   Read for: the causal claims it makes versus the evidence it presents. Bring the licensing table from `GLOSSARY-AND-DISTINCTIONS.md`.

2. **Survey** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990>
   Read for: how the survey talks about trust and assurance, and whether it, too, slides from evidence to causation.

# Assessments for Each Week

Five components, one rule: **the reasoning is graded, not the number.** A precise "unidentified" outscores a confident estimate with hidden assumptions.

---

## 1. Provenance audit report — 15%, due week 5 Saturday

One agent, one captured failure, two halves: what the graph explains, and what it cannot.

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Exemplary (90–100)** | Capture is reproducible from the repo; the graph is correct and minimal; the "unresolved" half names specific silences (claim support, invalidation, responsibility) with evidence from the trace; every inferred edge is marked as inferred |
| **Proficient (75–89)** | Capture works; the reconstruction is correct; the silences are listed but generic ("provenance does not capture semantics") |
| **Developing (60–74)** | Capture works but requires undocumented manual steps; the report narrates the trace rather than auditing it; inferred edges are indistinguishable from observed ones |
| **Inadequate (<60)** | No working capture, or the report claims the provenance graph explains the failure when it only describes the sequence |

Automatic fail: presenting an inferred edge as recorded.

## 2. Causal identification exercises — 20%, weekly, weeks 6–10

Five sets: DAG construction (w6), back-door adjustment (w7), counterfactual specification (w8), bounds (w9), licensing critique (w10).

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Exemplary** | DAG is defensible and says which edges are observed; adjustment set is justified variable by variable; counterfactual step names its functional-form assumption; bounds are computed and *reported*; every untestable assumption appears in the ledger |
| **Proficient** | Correct method, correct arithmetic, assumptions mostly stated; one or two unstated but inferable from the working |
| **Developing** | Correct method applied to the wrong graph, or correct graph with an adjustment set that includes a collider; assumptions promised but not written |
| **Inadequate** | Effect reported without identification strategy, or a causal conclusion drawn from a dependency graph |

### The two exemplars that define the marking

**Full marks:**

> The naive difference in means is 0.31. Under the Manski bounds the effect lies in [−0.18, 0.62]; that is, the data are consistent with a substantial positive effect, no effect, or a modest negative one. I cannot narrow this without assuming either monotonicity or random tool assignment, neither of which is plausible here because tool selection depends on the prompt. Reported as unidentified; the bounds are the finding.

**Zero:**

> Tool failures clearly cause the drop in task success (r = −0.62, p < 0.01). Provenance confirms the causal chain, since the failing tool `wasInformedBy` the retrieval step.

The second is not wrong arithmetic; it is a category error, and it is the error the course exists to prevent.

## 3. Causal audit of a provenance paper — 10%, week 10 Saturday

One page. Identify every point where dependency tracing is presented or read as causal inference.

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Exemplary** | Cites page/section for each instance; distinguishes rhetorical framing from technical claim; proposes the sentence the authors could have written instead |
| **Proficient** | Finds the main instances with locations; the proposed rewrites are vague |
| **Developing** | Finds one instance and generalises; or accuses the paper of a claim it does not make |
| **Inadequate** | Summary of the paper rather than an audit; or the audit itself conflates dependency with causation |

A charitable audit that finds nothing must argue why the paper is clean — that is a legitimate, difficult answer.

## 4. Final project — 40%, week 15

Repository, provenance export, notebook, four-page report, `ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

| Criterion | Weight | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Question and estimand | 5 | One question, stated so an intervention would answer it; the unit of analysis is explicit |
| Instrumentation | 8 | Reproducible capture; `agent_id` intact; a completeness checklist run and its gaps documented |
| Identification | 10 | DAG with observed/assumed edges marked; adjustment set justified; sensitivity or bounds; alternative explanations considered |
| Honesty | 8 | Untestable assumptions named; claims scoped to what the data licence; at least one claim explicitly withheld |
| Reproducibility | 5 | Fresh clone reproduces the analysis; provenance export included; seeds and versions pinned |
| Presentation | 4 | Ten minutes, no overrun, questions answered without retreating to jargon |

Automatic fail conditions: undisclosed LLM authorship of analysis prose; a causal claim unsupported by the identification strategy; a report with no limitations section.

## 5. Labs and peer review — 15%

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Exemplary** | Arrives prepared with a design sketch; critique names the specific assumption or query at fault; revises their own work in response and says what changed |
| **Proficient** | Participates substantively; critique is correct but general |
| **Developing** | Attends, contributes when asked, critique is "looks good" |
| **Inadequate** | Unprepared, or critique that attacks the person's competence rather than the graph |

Presence is not participation. The unit of credit is a critique someone could act on.

## Peer review protocol (weeks 12–14)

Three questions, in this order, no skipping:

1. **What does provenance establish here?** Name the observed edges and the claims they license.
2. **What does causal analysis add?** Name the estimand and the assumptions that make it identifiable — or state that it is not.
3. **What remains unidentified?** Name the assumption that cannot be tested with the data on hand, and what data would test it.

Reviewers write one paragraph per question. Authors respond in writing within 48 hours: accept, rebut, or defer, with a reason. The response is part of the grade for both parties.

## Late policy

- Labs: present or absent. No late marks; the lab is the practice, not the product.
- Written deliverables: −10% per day, floor 50%, because week 10's audit feeds the final project.
- Final project: extensions only for documented circumstances, requested before the deadline.

## Resubmission

One resubmission is allowed per written deliverable, within two weeks, capped at 85%. The resubmission must include a short note: what you changed and what you still cannot defend. The note is graded.

## Integrity, in one paragraph

You may use agents to write code, draft queries, and triage literature, and you must disclose it. You may not use them to author a causal claim you cannot defend in viva. The test is simple and unforgiving: if asked "why is this identified?", you must be able to answer without opening a chat log.
