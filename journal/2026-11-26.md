**Status:** v0.2, written 2026-11-22. To be revised, refactored and updated on a weekly-ish basis: the point throughout the project is to be almost constantly updating, changing and improving the plan.
**Reader:** me first, forkers second. A forker should internalise the gist of the AI-assisted *process* — including the need for constant revision, refactoring and tweaking based on lessons learned — then develop plans, with their own agents, to produce their own mixtape. If a sentence only makes sense to a forker, it is in the wrong document.

## 1. The thesis, in one paragraph

Causal engineering is the practice of doing causal inference — identification in the Pearl/Rubin tradition — **at scale**, on and with agentic systems, using automation to implement the concepts rather than merely discuss them. The field is currently good at two things separately: provenance systems record what happened, and identification methods estimate what would happen under intervention. Almost nobody is good at the join, and the join is where agentic systems actually fail: an output flows downstream, a claim propagates, a benchmark number is quoted as if it were an effect. This project is a personal instrument for occupying that join — a notebook that produces a poem, a tape, and a ledger row for every serious thing I read — published in a form someone else can fork. The audio is not the product. **The product is a habit of thought, made checkable.**

---

## 2. What this project is, stated six ways

1. **An instrument, not a show.** The deliverable is the notebook: sources, claims, contradictions, assumption rows. The tape is a rendering of the notebook into a form I will actually revisit.
2. **The unit of work is the claim-with-provenance**, not the episode. An episode is four to six claims, one live contradiction, one open question.
3. **The medium is a compression algorithm.** A poem cannot carry a literature review, so it forces a decision about what the claim actually is. That decision is the learning; the audio is the by-product.
4. **Repetition is the mechanism, not the format.** Six plays of one poem beat one play of six podcasts, because repetition plus sleep plus walking is where consolidation and incubation happen.
5. **The niche is the honesty layer.** Anyone can summarise an agent paper. Very few will say, out loud, in verse, "this is a dependency, not a cause; here is the assumption I cannot test."
6. **It must be forkable or it is a diary.** Forkable means: plain text, deterministic build, no proprietary service in the critical path, and a licence that permits reuse.

### The agent metaphor, used consistently

The project is easier to reason about if I use the vocabulary I already use for agents — and the journal entry already frames the study hour as "refreshing or making statefulness current".

| Agent concept | Project artefact |
|---|---|
| State | `claims.jsonl` + `ASSUMPTIONS.md` |
| Checkpoint | the weekly journal entry |
| Compressed representation | the poem (4–6 claims, 1 contradiction) |
| Replay | re-listening a tape |
| Diff | the assumption ledger's new rows, and the v1→v4 audio diff |
| Message queue | the reading pipeline (arXiv → alphaXiv → HF → notes) |
| Dead-letter queue | contradictions I could not resolve and parked |
| Observability | the listening log (one line per play) |

The point of the metaphor is not cuteness: it tells me what to instrument. Anything without a checkpoint is a rumour.

## 3. The subject: five workstreams of causal engineering at scale

"At scale" is doing real work in that sentence. It is not "causal inference, but bigger"; it is five distinct problems that only appear once agents and automation are in the loop.

**W1 — Identification under agentic data-generating processes.** The treatment is often a prompt, a retrieval step, or a tool choice; the outcome is a judgement, not a number. Confounding by context is the default, not the exception.

**W2 — Measurement and provenance infrastructure.** Flowcept, PROV-AGENT, OpenTelemetry: what gets recorded determines what can be asked later. The interesting design question is the *range* of transports — LMDB, MongoDB, Redis, RabbitMQ, Kafka, Mofka — and choosing by provenance need rather than by size of machinery. ([Flowcept](https://github.com/ORNL/flowcept) · [PROV-AGENT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866) · [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/))

**W3 — Automation of the causal workflow.** Agents proposing DAGs, suggesting adjustment sets, running refutations, flagging sensitivity. This is the "knowledge engineering" half: tools that implement concepts, not tools that talk about them.

**W4 — Multi-agent attribution and responsibility.** Who introduced the false claim, who amplified it, who failed to verify — and whether that question is a lookup or a counterfactual. It is almost always the latter.

**W5 — Evaluation of causal claims.** The field's dominant statistical error is not in the maths; it is quoting an observational benchmark as if it licensed a causal claim ("model X causes better reasoning"). A running theme, and a reliable source of contradictions. ([Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990) · [HF model cards](https://huggingface.co/models) · [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/))

Every episode should be placeable in one or more of W1–W5. If it is not, it belongs in the walk notes, not the tape.

## 4. The Riemann analogy, taken seriously

The comparison to AI-assisted work on the Riemann Hypothesis is useful precisely because it breaks in an instructive place.

**What transfers.** A capable reasoner plus tooling plus a team of engineers can reach further than the reasoner alone. The bottleneck moves from raw capability to *specification and verification* — deciding what would count as progress, and checking it.

**What does not transfer.** Mathematics has a verifier: a proof either checks or it does not. Causal engineering has no proof checker. An identification argument is valid only relative to a graph and a set of assumptions, and the graph is partly unobservable. So the field cannot import the mathematical model wholesale; it has to *build* its verifier.

That is the strategic insight this project organises around: **in causal engineering, the verifier is a documented assumption set plus an auditable dataflow.** Provenance gives you the dataflow. The ledger gives you the assumptions. Neither is sufficient alone, and the join is exactly what "causal engineering" should mean.

If the project has one intellectual commitment, it is that: build the verifier, in public, one poem at a time.

## 5. The three bets (and what would falsify them)

A project about causal honesty should state its own assumptions in falsifiable form.

| Bet | Claim | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | The binding constraint is shifting from capability to auditability: agents can already produce plausible causal analyses; the scarce skill is knowing which ones are licensed | By mid-2028, agent-produced causal reports routinely ship with identification audits and nobody needs a human to adjudicate them |
| **B2** | The durable artefact is the assumption set, not the result: results are re-derivable, assumptions are not | Tools emerge that infer and validate identification assumptions automatically from data, making the ledger redundant |
| **B3** | Scale changes the failure mode from bias to un-auditable complexity: the errors that matter are no longer statistical but structural (silent confounders, poisoned memory, unattributable multi-agent propagation) | Provenance + evaluation turn out to be sufficient for audit at scale, and the gap this project tracks closes |

None of these is safe. Writing them down is the only way to notice, in eighteen months, whether the project was early, late, or wrong.

## 6. Why audio, why poetry, why sleep and walking

Three listening states, three jobs. Confusing them is the most common way this kind of project fails.

| State | What the tape is for | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Drift** (lying down, lights off) | Priming and consolidation of what was reviewed awake; holding one open question | Low-arousal, mono, quiet, no new information after halfway |
| **Walk** (moving, recording) | Incubation and capture; the poem as a prompt for my own thinking | Full band, chaptered, one question to carry |
| **3 a.m.** (woken, briefly) | Re-anchoring without arousal | Twelve-minute refrain tape, no navigation |

Two honesty notes, because this is a project about not overclaiming:

- **Hypnopedia is a myth.** Learning new material during sleep is not supported. What is supported is that sleep consolidates material *encoded before* sleep, and that cues associated with that material can bias reactivation. The tape's job is to cue, not to teach.
- **The poem is not decoration.** It is the only form I have found that forces a claim down to its load-bearing noun and its unresolved question. Prose lets me hide behind qualifications; a refrain does not.

## 7. The knowledge architecture

One corpus, many renderings. This is the strategic version of tactic #1, and it is the reason the project can survive a busy month: nothing is produced twice.

```
arXiv / alphaXiv / HF
        │  capture (hash, date, licence)
        ▼
   corpus/index.jsonl ──► claims.jsonl ──► contradictions.md
        │                     │                  │
        │                     ▼                  ▼
        │              ASSUMPTIONS.md      poem (4–6 anchors, 1 question)
        │                     │                  │
        ▼                     ▼                  ▼
   journal entry ◄──── listening log ◄──── tape (drift / walk / 3am)
        │
        ▼
  (optional renderings: book chapter, course exercise, forkable edition)
```

Rules that keep it honest:

- Every anchor term in a poem traces to a claim id; every claim has a source hash and a date.
- Contradictions are first-class: parked, dated, and revisited, never smoothed away.
- The journal entry is the checkpoint; if it is missing, the episode did not happen, whatever the audio says.
- Audio is a *derived artefact*: it can be re-rendered from text, never the reverse.

## 8. The publication contract (what a fork inherits)

The repo is the artefact; the audio is an attachment. A fork should be able to produce its own tapes without my voice, my music, or my opinions, so the contract has to be explicit.

| Inherited | Not inherited | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `claims.jsonl`, `sources.yml`, `contradictions.md` | My voice model | Voice cloning is per-person and non-transferable by design |
| Poem texts and journal entries | Suno beds | Beds are licensed to the account, not the project; document the prompt so a fork can regenerate |
| Build scripts, render presets, loudness chain | Listening log | Personal by nature |
| Voice *bible* (settings, not the voice) | Episode ordering | Order is a teaching decision, not a truth |

Proposed licensing, to be confirmed before the first public release:

- **Text** (poems, journal, claims): [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) — maximal reuse, attribution only.
- **Audio**: [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) — reuse and remix, no commercial use, because a fork selling my sleep tape is a bad outcome.
- **Code**: MIT.

And one non-negotiable engineering rule, inherited from the book sprint: **no proprietary service in the critical path.** A fork must be able to build the text artefacts with `make` and no accounts.

## 9. Roadmap

Dates assume the current start (23 November 2026) and the existing cadences: the 06:00 workshop, the 09:30 study hour, the 12:30–17:30 sprint. The podcast is the *fourth* activity, so the roadmap is deliberately slow — a project that competes for attention loses.

### Phase 0 — Instrument (Nov–Dec 2026, 4 weeks)

Build the pipes, produce nothing publishable. Voice bible, three beds, loudness chain, two render presets, `claims.jsonl` seeded from the course, one poem written and thrown away.

**Exit criteria:** one 20-minute drift tape and one 20-minute walk tape produced end-to-end in under three hours total; a fresh clone of the repo rebuilds both from text.

### Phase 1 — Cadence (Jan–Mar 2027, 12 weeks)

One poem and one tape per week, for twelve weeks. Goal is not quality; it is proving the loop survives a bad week, a cold, and a holiday.

**Exit criteria:** 12 tapes; median production time ≤ 2 h; ≥ 8 of 12 have a live contradiction; the listening log has ≥ 30 entries.

### Phase 2 — Depth (Apr–Sep 2027, 26 weeks)

The corpus becomes the point. Two sources a week, one workstream focus per month (W1 → W5), and the first public edition of the notebook. Poems get better because the contradictions get sharper.

**Exit criteria:** 150+ claims with primary sources; a contradiction map with ≥ 20 live tensions; 26 tapes; one outsider completes a fork and produces a tape (the fork test, borrowed from the book sprint).

### Phase 3 — Synthesis (Oct 2027–Mar 2028)

A single long-form artefact: an eight-chapter audio "state of causal engineering", assembled from the year's tapes and rewritten, not concatenated. The chapter structure is the one in `BOOK-SKELETON.md`, adapted for audio.

**Exit criteria:** eight chapters, each with a bibliography page in the notebook and an explicit assumptions section; total runtime ≤ 4 hours; a listener can answer "what is unresolved in this field?" from the tapes alone.

### Phase 4 — Fork ecosystem (2028+)

Optional, and only if Phase 3 lands. The notebook becomes usable by someone else as a course or a reading group; the tapes become a reference rather than a diary.

**Kill criterion for the whole project:** if by the end of Phase 2 I have not re-listened to any tape more than three times, the medium is wrong for me and the project should collapse back into a written notebook without apology.

## 10. Metrics that matter, and the ones that do not

| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| Claims with a primary source (%) | The only defence against vibes |
| Contradictions stated, and still open | The engine of the whole thing |
| Assumption rows added per month | Proxy for thinking, not reading |
| Median time from reading to tape | Measures whether the loop is sustainable |
| Re-listens per tape | The actual point of the medium |
| Tapes retired | Honesty about what did not work |

| Ignore | Why |
|---|---|
| Downloads, plays, followers | Not the audience; vanity with extra steps |
| Episode count | Encourages volume over compression |
| Audio "quality" scores | The listener is me, on a pillow, at 2 a.m. |
| Citation counts of sources | Popularity is not evidence |

## 11. Failure modes and guardrails

| Failure | How it starts | Guardrail | Kill signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Summariser drift** | The poem becomes a recap of the abstract | Every poem must contain one contradiction and one question | A tape with no contradiction is deleted, not published |
| **Music project** | Suno is more fun than reading | Three beds per quarter; no new bed until one is retired | You have spent more time in Suno than in papers this month |
| **Second job** | Production creeps: more takes, more polish | 90-minute cap; one render chain; two voices | Median production time exceeds 4 h |
| **Vibes drift** | Following the nose out of causal engineering into generic AI commentary | The W1–W5 placement test | An episode you cannot place |
| **Podcast-voice trap** | Performing for an imaginary audience | No performance register; no "welcome back" | You hear yourself smiling |
| **Sleep dependency** | The tape becomes necessary to fall asleep | 45-minute timer, no autoplay, quarterly check | You cannot sleep without it |
| **Perfectionism** | v7 of a poem nobody will hear twice | Ship v1, listen once, revise once | You have not published in three weeks |

Two of these (summariser drift, vibes drift) are the ones that will actually kill the project. The rest are annoyances.

The tactics are logistics; the strategy is which logistics matter and when. Mapping:

| Tactic | Strategic function | Phase | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Weekly, batched | Makes the loop sustainable | 1 | B2, whole project |
| 2 Per-stanza takes | Quality without re-editing | 1 | Enjoyment of the artefact |
| 3 Three beds/quarter | Consistency as a cue; kills the music project | 0 | Failure mode "music project" |
| 4 Frozen voice bible | Reproducibility of the rendering | 0 | Forkability |
| 5 90-minute cap | Protects the rest of the day | 1 | Failure mode "second job" |
| 6 4–6 anchors tied to claims | Forces the claim decision | 1 | B2, honesty layer |
| 7 Refrain is the question | Keeps the tape alive on re-listen | 1 | Incubation |
| 8 One contradiction per paper | Converts reading into material | 2 | Contradiction map |
| 9 Rotate the angle | Repetition without boredom | 2 | Consolidation |
| 10 A form that cannot conclude | Structure that resists summarising | 1 | Failure mode "summariser drift" |
| 11 Fixed 30-second ritual | Conditioning; state change on demand | 0 | Drift state |
| 12 Drift mix spec | Physiological correctness | 0 | Drift state |
| 13 Walk mix spec | Separation of listening states | 0 | Walk state |
| 14 Bed as weather | Protects sleep onset | 0 | Drift state |
| 15 No new info after 30:00 | Respects what sleep can do | 1 | Honesty about hypnopedia |
| 16 Fade, never stop | Avoids startle; permits looping | 0 | Drift state |
| 17 Two voices, one asks | Occupies the mind without engaging it | 1 | Compressed disagreement |
| 18 Timer, no autoplay | Bounds exposure and dependency | 1 | Failure mode "sleep dependency" |
| 19 Three lines before coffee | Captures the hypnagogic residue | 1 | Corpus growth |
| 20 Local transcription | Keeps unfinished thinking local | 2 | Data sovereignty |
| 21 Versioned renders | The diff is the learning record | 1 | B2 |
| 22 NotebookLM on public sources | Discovery without egress | 2 | Failure mode "vibes drift" |
| 23 restic backups | Durability of the only irreplaceable asset | 0 | Forkability |
| 24 Volume limits | Long-term capacity to listen at all | 0 | Sustainability |
| 25 The 3 a.m. tape | Highest-yield cue moment | 2 | Drift state |

**What the tactics do not cover, and probably should** — five candidates for a second list:

- **26. A contradiction triage rule.** When two sources disagree, decide within a week: parked, resolved, or promoted to a poem. Otherwise the contradiction map becomes a graveyard.
- **27. A source-quality gate.** Primary documents before secondary summaries; a paper without a methods section is a press release.
- **28. A quarterly strategic review.** Re-read this document, revise the bets, kill a tactic, add one.
- **29. A voice-cloning consent note.** If I ever clone my voice, record the decision and its constraints in the repo; a fork must not inherit a synthetic me by accident.
- **30. A fork test.** Once a year, someone else builds a tape from the repo alone. If they cannot, the publication contract is fiction.

## 13. What success looks like in 24 months

Not exactly "a podcast with listeners" — something much, much narrower and more useful to the individual producing the learning notebook:

- A notebook with 150+ claims, each with a hash-stamped source and a date, in which the contradictions are easier to find than the conclusions.
- Eight to twelve tapes I still re-listen to, because they hold a question rather than an answer.
- A documented assumption ledger that would let a stranger — or a future me — reconstruct why I believed what I believed in 2027.
- The habit that makes everything else possible: reading papers as material for compression, and hearing my own overclaims before anyone else has to.
- One forker who built a tape from the repo alone: not an audience metric, but proof that the process is transmissible.

And a stop condition, stated now so it is not negotiated later: **if the notebook is not better than the tapes, the tapes are the wrong artefact.**

## Appendix C — Quarterly review questions (25)

Ask them in order, slowly, and write the answers rather than thinking them. Most will not have an obvious answer today; that is the point — they are here to be carried, not resolved.

**Epistemic**

1. Which of B1–B3 would embarrass me in eighteen months, and what specifically would make it embarrassing?
2. What is the unstated causal claim behind this project — that listening to poems changes how I reason — and what would I accept as evidence for or against it?
3. Which assumption in `ASSUMPTIONS.md` has never been tested and never will be, and why is it still taking up a row?
4. Which contradiction did I resolve by losing interest rather than by evidence?
5. Where have I confused "I can explain this" with "this is true", and which tape would need re-recording if I fixed it?

**Medium**

6. If the poem is the compression, what has compression destroyed that I no longer notice?
7. What would be lost if the audio disappeared tomorrow — what does the text not contain?
8. What is the marginal value of the sixth re-listen over the first, and have I ever measured it rather than assumed it?
9. Which listening state produces the useful notes (drift, walk, or 3 a.m.), and is the design actually optimised for that one?
10. Where has the medium selected for material — papers that poemify well — rather than for importance?

**Process**

11. Which source did I cite but never read past the abstract, and is it still in the corpus?
12. Which tactic did I quietly abandon, and did abandoning it improve or degrade the work?
13. What is the half-life of a claim here? Have I ever retracted one, and what did the retraction cost?
14. Is the forkability claim real, or has nobody but me ever built a tape — and if they tried, what did their attempt reveal about the contract?
15. Which part of the pipeline is proprietary in practice, despite the "no proprietary service in the critical path" rule?

**Economics and incentives**

16. What does this project do that a well-organised reference manager and a weekly walk would not?
17. Which workstream (W1–W5) is actually growing, and which is a label I apply to whatever I happened to read?
18. What am I optimising for when I choose a source: importance, contradiction density, or ease of rendering? Which one should it be?
19. What would this cost per useful claim if I priced my hours honestly, and is that price acceptable?
20. If a funder or an employer asked what this is for, what is the honest one-sentence answer — and does it match §1?

**Self-deception**

21. Which term do I use because I understand it, and which because it signals membership?
22. Which poem have I kept because it sounds good rather than because it is right?
23. Which question in the tapes is still unanswered after a quarter — and is it unanswered because it is hard, or because I never worked on it?
24. What would a causal inference professor attack first in my strongest tape, and do I already know the answer?
25. What condition would make me stop, and am I honest enough to notice when it is met rather than renegotiating it?
